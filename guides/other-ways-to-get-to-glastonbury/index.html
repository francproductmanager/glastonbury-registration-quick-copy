Missed out? Other ways to get to Glastonbury

Your realistic options if you didn't get a ticket: the spring resale, volunteering, working, and what to avoid.

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 26 October 2026.

Most people who try for Glastonbury tickets don't get them, so if you missed out you're in good company. There are still a few genuine routes in. None is guaranteed, and each has its own trade-offs.

1. The spring resale

The obvious second chance. Tickets that weren't paid for in the April balance window, and tickets that were returned, are sold again in the spring. It's usually in late April, with a coach resale first and a general admission resale a few days later. It's smaller and faster than the main sale, and tickets are paid in full rather than with a deposit. See how the spring resale works.

2. Volunteering

The festival's partner charities, including Oxfam, WaterAid and Greenpeace, recruit volunteers to work shifts in return for entry, crew camping and meals. Applications for 2027 open in the new year, and places go fast. Each charity's dates, shifts and terms are in volunteering at Glastonbury 2027.

3. Paid work

Traders, bars, security firms and contractors hire staff for the festival. These roles usually ask for more experience than volunteering and give you less free time, but you're paid. Jobs come through the companies themselves and staffing agencies, not the festival.

4. Sunday tickets for local residents

The festival has offered a limited number of Sunday-only tickets to people who live locally, with their own terms and conditions. They're not a general sale: if you don't live in the qualifying area, they're not a route for you. If you do, watch the festival's own announcements, as the details can change from year to year.

5. Charity competitions

Some of the festival's partner charities run competitions with festival tickets as prizes. Greenpeace, for example, has a page for winning Glastonbury tickets. Enter only through the charity's own website. A post or message saying you've won, that then asks for a payment or your registration details, is a scam.

6. Coach packages next time

If you only tried general admission, try the coach sale too next time. It's a separate sale a few days earlier, so it gives your group an extra chance. See coach tickets.

What not to do

  • Don't buy from anyone other than See Tickets. Tickets carry the holder's photo and can't be transferred, so a ticket bought from someone else won't get you in.
  • Don't pay anyone to "change the name" or photo on a ticket. It can't be done that way.
  • Don't share your registration details with strangers who say they'll buy for you. Only book with people you know.

See how to spot Glastonbury ticket scams.

A realistic plan if you missed out

  1. Now: decide as a group whether you'll try the resale together, and whether anyone wants to apply to volunteer.
  2. Winter: check everyone's registration and update old photos while registration is open.
  3. Early in the new year: watch the charities' volunteering pages, as places can go quickly.
  4. April: the resale. Plan bookings of up to four people, with the full price ready.

Keep ready for next time

Whatever you try, keep your registration up to date, keep your group's details together and agree a plan early. When the next sale comes round, you'll be ready in minutes rather than days.
