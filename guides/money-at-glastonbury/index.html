Do you need cash at Glastonbury? Money on site explained

Cash or card at Glastonbury: which bars and food stalls take cards, the 24 hour cash machines, what food and drink cost, Challenge 21, and keeping money safe.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 21 November 2026.

5 min read

Yes, bring some cash. The festival says over twenty of its main bars take debit and credit cards, and many food traders do too, but not all of them can, "so please expect to use cash over the weekend". If you run out, there are cash machines in three places around the site, open 24 hours a day, taking all UK and international cards.

Everything here is from the advice and facilities sections of the festival's information page, read in full on 21 November 2026. What the trip costs before you get there, from the ticket to parking, is in what Glastonbury 2027 costs.

Card or cash: where you can use each

WhereCard?What the festival says
The main barsYesOver twenty of them take debit and credit cards, on more than 100 card readers
Food stallsMany, not allNot all traders can take cards, so expect to use cash
Market stallsNot statedThe page doesn't say, so carry cash to be safe
Cash machinesYour cardThree locations, open 24 hours, all UK and international cards accepted

The information page doesn't mention paying by phone or contactless on site, so we can't say whether every card reader takes them. Bring your physical card as well as your phone.

Using the cash machines

The festival's advice for the cash machines is short and practical:

  • Go with a friend, for company and security, especially after dark.
  • Check your PIN before you leave home, and bring the debit or credit card that goes with it.
  • Take only the cards you need. Make a note of your card numbers and leave it at home, with the number to call if a card goes missing.

If you're coming from abroad, the machines take international cards. More for visitors is in buying Glastonbury tickets from abroad.

What food and drink cost

The festival doesn't publish a price list, but its information page gives two useful pointers:

  • Food. Its food section says that "this year" 300 stallholders, over 75 percent of all traders, offered a £6 meal deal, marked with a sticker on their menu boards, with options from breakfast to curry, a kids meal and drink, or coffee and cakes for two. The page doesn't say which year that was, and 2027's traders haven't been told yet whether they have a pitch, so check again nearer the festival.
  • Drink. The beer tents are run by the Workers Beer Company and Avalon bars, and the festival says their prices are "equivalent to London pub prices".

There are over 400 food traders across the site, including many vegetarian and vegan stalls, and many can cater for specific dietary needs.

What's free

Some things cost nothing, which helps any budget:

  • Drinking water from the free taps at more than 850 water points, including WaterAid water bars. Bring a reusable bottle.
  • Phone charging, which the festival lists as part of what a ticket includes.
  • Loo rolls, site maps, ear plugs and squirts of sunscreen from the Info Points, and loo rolls from the Property Lock-ups too.

Buying alcohol: Challenge 21

The festival runs a Challenge 21 policy. If you look under 21, you won't be served alcohol unless you can prove you're over 18, either by showing ID or by wearing a Challenge 21 wristband.

  • Getting a wristband. The Property Lock-ups and the Info Points hand them out when you show valid photo ID.
  • Which ID. A UK driving licence, a valid passport, or a PASS ID card if you'd rather not bring either. The festival says original documents are needed for Challenge 21, not photocopies.

If you bring your own alcohol, the festival says amounts beyond reasonable personal use will be confiscated at the gate, and so will glass bottles: decant drinks into other containers before you travel.

Keeping your money safe

The festival's advice on keeping money and valuables safe, from several parts of its information page:

  • Don't keep all your cash in one place. Spread it between pockets, and when you sleep, split money and valuables into small amounts hidden in different places in your tent.
  • Use the Property Lock-ups. There are 14 around the site, free and open 24 hours, from 8am on the Wednesday to noon on the Monday. You can leave credit cards, wallets, passports and travel tickets there for as long as you like. Anything left after they close has to be claimed from Worthy Welfare, possibly not until after 4pm.
  • Keep valuables out of sight, in a front pocket if possible.
  • Only bring what you can afford to lose.

For keeping things safe at your tent, see where to camp at Glastonbury.

If you lose your wallet or cards

  1. Cancel your cards. The Info Points hold the phone numbers for cancelling lost bank cards, and have a landline for emergency calls.
  2. Check lost property. It's at Worthy Welfare, open 24 hours during the festival. The festival says over 700 items were handed in at the last festival, including 250 wallets and cardholders with their contents.
  3. Report a theft. The police compound is at the top of the Big Ground camping field, and the police can give you a statement for insurance. For non-emergencies you can also call 101.

Planning how much to bring

The festival doesn't suggest a daily budget, and we haven't found a source that does, so we won't guess one. A simple way to plan: count the meals you'll buy over five days, add drinks and anything you want from the markets, and decide how much of it you'll pay by card. Then bring enough cash for the rest, knowing the cash machines are there if you run short. The cost guide has a table for the costs you set yourself.

What we couldn't confirm

The information page doesn't say whether the cash machines charge a fee, whether contactless or phone payments work everywhere cards do, or which year its meal deal figures are from. We'll update this guide when the festival publishes its 2027 details.
