Common Glastonbury ticket day mistakes

The small slips that cost people tickets or time every year, why they happen, and how to avoid each one.

By the maker of Glasto Quick Copy (about us). Facts checked 20 October 2026.

Most ticket day disappointment is down to the queue. But every year some groups get through and still lose time or places to avoidable slips. Almost all of them can be fixed days before the sale.

1. Someone isn't registered

Or their photo was rejected and nobody noticed. People often assume a registration from an earlier year still works without checking, or submit a photo and never open the email that follows.

Fix: ask everyone to forward their latest confirmation email well before the day, and check it says the photo was accepted. See how registration works.

2. The wrong postcode

The postcode must be the one the person registered with. Students, people who've recently moved and anyone who registered at a parent's address are the usual cases.

Fix: when you collect details, ask "which postcode is on your registration?", not "what's your postcode?".

3. A typo in a registration number

Typing ten digits under time pressure is easy to get wrong, and swapping two digits is the classic slip. It's also easy to copy a number from an old email.

Fix: copy and paste instead of typing, and double check the names that appear before paying.

4. Hunting through the group chat

Numbers scattered across weeks of messages are slow to find, and in a busy chat it's easy to copy the wrong person's number. Glasto Quick Copy is built for exactly this.

Fix: collect everything in one place beforehand, with one entry per person.

5. Two people booking the same friends

When several people queue, two of them can get through at similar times and both book the same group. One booking then has to be refunded, and someone who could have booked others has used their turn.

Fix: agree who covers whom, and announce bookings the moment they're confirmed. See the worked example in buying tickets for a group.

6. Too many tabs

In recent sales, extra tabs and devices with the same registration number didn't help, and refreshing could lose your place. Lots of tabs also slow down older laptops and phones.

Fix: one device and one tab per registration number. Let the page move on by itself.

7. A card that gets declined

A lead booker paying deposits for six people is making a bigger payment than usual, at a moment when a lot of people are paying at once. Some banks treat that as unusual.

Fix: check your available balance and limit beforehand, and have a second card ready just in case.

8. A phone that locks or dies

Waiting in a queue can take a long time. A phone that locks, goes to sleep or runs out of battery mid-queue is a needless risk.

Fix: charge it, plug it in and stop it locking. The tool's Keep screen on option helps once you're on your group's page.

9. Forgetting the balance

Getting a deposit isn't the end. Balances are due months later, when the excitement has worn off.

Fix: put the balance payment dates in your calendar straight away, and ask everyone in the group to do the same. See deposits and balance payments.

10. Falling for a resale scam

After a sell-out, posts appear offering spare tickets. Glastonbury tickets carry the holder's photo and can't be transferred.

Fix: only buy through the official resale. See resale and coach tickets.
