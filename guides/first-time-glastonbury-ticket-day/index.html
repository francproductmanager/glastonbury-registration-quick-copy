Your first go at Glastonbury tickets: what ticket morning is like

A plain walk through a Glastonbury sale for first timers, from registering to the confirmation page, with what you'll see and how long each part took in 2027.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 26 October 2026.

4 min read

If you've never tried for Glastonbury tickets, the stories from friends can make ticket morning sound like chaos: crashing pages, endless refreshing, a group chat in meltdown. In reality it's a handful of steps, most of which happen weeks before the sale. This guide walks through them in order, using the 2027 sale as the example, so you know what to expect and what's normal.

Weeks before: everyone registers

You can't buy a ticket, or be booked by a friend, without a registration. It's free, done on See Tickets, and needs a recent photo of your face, which is checked by a person and later printed on your ticket. The festival says the check takes about 48 hours in the working week, and you get an email with your registration number once it's accepted.

Registration closes before each sale. For 2027 it closed at 5pm on Friday 25 September 2026, a week before the first sale. If you leave it to the last few days and your photo is rejected, you may run out of time to try again. See how registration works.

A week or two before: who books whom

One person, the lead booker, can buy up to six tickets in one go: their own and up to five friends'. For each friend they need two things: the friend's registration number and the postcode on their registration. So you don't need to reach the front yourself to get a ticket. Someone in your group can book you.

But if you're registered and free at sale time, you should queue too. Each registration gets its own place in the queue, so every extra person queueing is an extra chance for the whole group. Most groups agree that whoever gets through first books everyone. See buying tickets for a group.

The two sales

There are usually two sales a few days apart. For 2027, ticket and coach packages went on sale at 6pm on Thursday 1 October 2026, and general admission tickets at 9am on Sunday 4 October. The coach sale is an extra chance, but the ticket comes with a fixed return coach journey, so agree as a group before trying it. See coach tickets.

What the queue looks like

Mostly, nothing happens. You'll watch a page that moves slowly or seems not to move at all, sometimes for a long time. That's normal, and it doesn't mean the site has crashed or you've lost your place. Leave it alone: refreshing, extra tabs and extra devices don't help. What each screen means, and what to do if one looks wrong, is in how the queue works.

What happens at the front

If you get through, the booking page opens and a timer starts. You enter your own details and each friend's registration number and postcode, check that the names shown are the people you expect, then pay a deposit for each ticket: £100 per ticket for 2027. Tickets aren't yours until the payment has gone through.

When the confirmation page appears, screenshot it and tell your group straight away, so nobody else books the same people. The timers, the cards that work and what to do if something fails are all in the checkout guide.

How it usually ends

Be ready for this: most people who try don't get a ticket. Demand is many times the number of tickets, and every recent sale has sold out in under an hour. If you see "SOLD OUT", every ticket in that sale has gone, and there's nothing you did wrong. Close the page, and turn your attention to the spring resale.

Things first timers often don't know

  • Arriving early matters, but only up to the start. In 2027, everyone in the waiting room before 9am got a random place. Being there at 8.20 instead of 8.50 didn't put you further forward.
  • Reaching the front isn't the same as having a ticket. Tickets are only allocated once payment goes through.
  • Tickets can't be passed on. Each one carries the holder's photo, so the only ticket that works is one booked against your own registration.
  • The deposit isn't the whole price. For 2027 the ticket is £408, with the £308 balance due between 1 and 7 April 2027. Miss that window and the ticket is cancelled.
  • You don't have to be online to be booked. If a friend gets through, they can book you with your number and postcode, wherever you are.

After the morning

If you got a ticket, your next job is the balance in April. See what to do between now and June. If you didn't, you're in the majority. Keep your registration up to date and read how the spring resale works.

The calmest people on ticket morning are the ones whose group sorted everything a week earlier. If you only do one thing, get registered early and send your number and registered postcode to whoever's organising.
