Glastonbury Festival jobs: paid work and how to apply

Paid jobs at Glastonbury Festival: when it recruits, the 2027 roles on its website, the pay, the right to work in the UK, the clear-up crew and trader jobs.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 18 November 2026.

6 min read

Glastonbury Festival hires temporary staff every year, and recruiting for 2027 has started. Its information page says most departments begin recruiting in the late autumn and into the early new year, for jobs starting in the spring, and that roles are advertised on the festival's own website first. You need the right to work in the UK. This guide covers the paid jobs the festival lists, what they pay, how to apply, and the other paid work around the festival.

Everything here is from the Jobs section of the festival's information page and the job sheets it links to, read in full on 18 November 2026. Volunteering for a ticket is a different route, covered in volunteering at Glastonbury 2027.

The festival's job sheets don't mention a festival ticket for any of the jobs listed, so don't apply expecting one. For the clear-up crew it says plainly that no ticket comes with the job.

How many people work at Glastonbury?

Fewer people work for the festival full time than you might think. The information page says the full time staff are a relatively small team, largely in administrative roles such as finance, licensing, customer services, site design and ground works. Its economic impact summary for the 2023 festival gives the scale of the rest:

  • Around 80 people worked in the festival office on planning and administration, most of them living locally.
  • Another 1,750 people worked directly for the festival over shorter periods.
  • The 918 trading stalls were staffed by 9,500 people, nearly all working for independent businesses.
  • In all, the festival sustained over 1,100 UK jobs, 325 of them in Somerset.

The jobs advertised for 2027

When we checked on 18 November 2026, the information page listed seven fixed-term jobs for the 2027 festival, each with its own job sheet. All are based at the festival's offices at Worthy Farm in Pilton, and all involve some weekend work around the festival.

Pay on these seven sheets is £14.50 to £19 an hour. The contracts fall between 1 February and 16 July 2027, and the closing dates run from 20 November to 8 December 2026. Each sheet gives its own dates and rate, so open the one you want before you apply. The festival says to keep an eye on its vacancies list for updates.

How to apply

  1. Open the job sheet from the Jobs section of the festival's information page, and check the dates, hours and anything it requires, like a driving licence.
  2. Check you have the right to work in the UK. The festival says you must have it, and that people from outside the UK need a work permit. Because most roles are short, it says its ability to get a UK work permit for someone from outside the UK is extremely limited.
  3. Email your CV and a covering letter to the festival's HR team at hr@glastonburyfestivals.co.uk before the closing date on the sheet. That is how every sheet asks you to apply.
  4. Ask for any adjustments you need. The festival says it is an equal opportunities employer, and to let it know if you need reasonable adjustments to apply or once employed.

Think about getting there, too. The festival points out that its offices are in rural Somerset, with limited public transport outside the festival period, and several of these jobs start months before the festival.

If you need the festival's access provision while working there, it has a separate Crew Accessibility Application. It asks you to request it in the new year, and only once your job is confirmed.

Is it a way into the festival?

Not an easy one. The festival says none of these jobs is an easy way in: they involve hard work and not much time off over the festival period. It also says festival or events experience can help, but isn't essential.

Other paid work

The post-festival clear-up crew

The clear-up crew does the litter pick after the festival. The festival describes it as short term, casual paid work, with numbers that can change with demand and things like the weather. No festival ticket comes with it, but you can do it whether you have a ticket or not. You must be 18 or over. The festival hasn't said yet how to apply for 2027, only to keep an eye on the Jobs section.

Working for a trader

The festival says market traders need staff. If you know traders who come to the festival, contact them early: each stall has only a limited number of passes. Traders apply separately to the festival for their pitches, and their applications for 2027 have closed.

Work experience

You can ask about a work experience placement, but the festival says to give the dates you're available, and that placements often aren't possible because its team is only on the festival site for a short time.

Watch out for fake job offers

The festival warns about bogus schemes where people claiming to recruit for festival work ask for a deposit to secure a place. If you're not sure a company is genuine, it says to contact the festival at help@glastonburyfestivals.co.uk before you pay. Genuine festival jobs are advertised on its own website first. For the signs of a scam, see how to spot Glastonbury ticket scams.

If you're after a ticket rather than a wage

If what you want is to see the festival, the spring resale and volunteering are the other routes in: see other ways to get to Glastonbury and volunteering at Glastonbury 2027.

What we couldn't confirm

The job sheets we read don't say whether staff get a festival ticket, crew camping or meals, so we haven't said either way. The festival hasn't published how to apply for the 2027 clear-up crew yet. We'll update this guide as the vacancies change.
