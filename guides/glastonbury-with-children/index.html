Taking children and teenagers to Glastonbury: tickets and registration

How old you have to be to go to Glastonbury, who needs a ticket, the rules for under 16s and teenagers, coach seats for children and booking a family group.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 31 October 2026.

5 min read

Glastonbury is a family festival for a lot of people, and children are well catered for, with the Kidzfield area included in the ticket. But the ticket rules depend on age in a way that trips families up, especially with teenagers.

How old do you have to be to go to Glastonbury?

The rules change at 13 (your age on the day the gates open) and at 16. This is what the festival's information page says for 2027:

AgeTicket and registrationCan they go without an adult?
12 or underNo ticket and no registration neededNo: they must come with a ticket holding adult
13, 14 or 15Their own registration and a TEEN ticketNo: a responsible adult must be with them
16 or 17Their own registration and a ticketYes, though the festival strongly advises a parent or guardian agrees
18 or overTheir own registration and a ticketYes

A responsible adult is someone aged 18 or over, usually the parent, carer or legal guardian, who accepts full responsibility for, and supervises, every under 16 in their care at all times. Anyone under 16 can't enter, leave or re-enter the site without their responsible adult.

Teenagers aged 13 to 15 can get a Parental Permission for Exit/Re-entry Wristband, which lets them leave and come back in. It is collected from the public festival entrances or the Info Points, and the responsible adult and the teenager must both be there to collect it.

For children who don't need a ticket, you can pick up a child wristband from the Kidzfield or the Info Points once you are inside. And anyone on an adult ticket who looks under 16 is advised to bring ID that shows their age.

The age rule for tickets

Anyone who will be 13 or over on the day the gates open needs their own ticket and their own registration. Children who are 12 or under on that day don't need a ticket and don't register.

For 2027 the gates open on Wednesday 23 June, so anyone born on or before 23 June 2014 needs a ticket. A child who is 12 on ticket day in October but turns 13 before 23 June needs one.

Registering a teenager

A teenager registers exactly like an adult, with their own photo, and needs an email address. Their photo is printed on their ticket and checked at the gate, so use a recent one. Teenagers change fast, so it's worth updating the photo every year before the sale.

On ticket day they take one of the six places in a booking, with their own registration number and registered postcode. In a family, the registered postcode is usually the same for everyone, which makes it easy, but check it really is: a teenager who registered at another parent's address will have a different one.

Children on coaches

Children aged 12 and under don't need a festival ticket, but they do need a coach seat if you travel by ticket and coach package. For 2027 the festival says to book those seats while making the ticket and coach booking. If they aren't available then, you contact the See Tickets coach team after booking: extra seats are at standard fares and depend on availability. The coach tickets guide has the rest of the coach rules.

Booking a family group

  • Count only the people who need tickets: everyone 13 or over on the gate opening day.
  • If the family plus friends comes to more than six, split into booking groups and keep each child's parent in the same booking as any teenager they're responsible for.
  • Have the parents queue on separate devices with their own registrations for more chances.
  • Put everyone's details on one page before the day, including the teenagers'.

Questions families ask

Does my teenager need to be there on ticket day?

No. Whoever gets through the queue books them with their registration number and registered postcode, exactly like any other member of the group. They don't need to be online, but they do need to be registered with an accepted photo before registration closes.

Can a teenager queue for the family?

Anyone with their own registration can join the queue, which gives your family an extra chance. If you'd rather an adult pays, agree that a teenager who gets through hands over to a parent on the same device, or that the parent's card is to hand. Remember the clock is running once you're at the front.

What about teenagers going without their parents?

It depends on their age. A 13, 14 or 15 year old must be booked on the TEEN ticket option, which confirms they will come with a responsible adult who has a ticket, and TEEN ticket holders are not let in on their own. Whoever books for them confirms they are 18 or over and accepts responsibility for every under 16 in the group, and confirms a parent, carer or legal guardian has agreed to their details being used. A 16 or 17 year old can go without an adult, though the festival strongly advises it is with a parent or guardian's agreement.

The group booking guide explains how bookings of up to six work, and how registration works covers getting their registration and photo in place before the sale.

We have a baby or toddler. Do we need to do anything?

Not for tickets: children 12 and under don't need one. You'll still want to look at the festival's family information nearer the time, and if you travel by coach, book them a seat.

What's included for children

The festival lists free Kidzfield entertainment, rides and activities as part of what a ticket includes. Check the official site nearer the time for family camping and the latest information.
