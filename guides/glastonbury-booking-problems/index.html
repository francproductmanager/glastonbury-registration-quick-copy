Glastonbury booking gone wrong? What to do if checkout fails

What to do when a name doesn't come up, a registration is locked, the timer runs out, a card is declined or the confirmation doesn't arrive, based on the official sale FAQs.

By the maker of Glasto Quick Copy (about us). Facts checked 26 October 2026.

Getting through the queue and then watching the booking go wrong is the worst feeling of ticket day. It happens to some people every year, and most problems have a sensible next step if you know what you're looking at. This guide goes through them one at a time. Where the rules come from a particular year's official FAQ, we say which.

First, tell your group

Whatever goes wrong, post one short message straight away, like "FAILED, still need Group A". Someone else in your group may still be in the queue, and they need to know the people you were booking aren't covered yet. Then sort out the problem. See messages to send your Glastonbury group.

A friend's name doesn't come up

When you enter a registration number and postcode, the booking page shows the person's name. If it shows nothing, or someone you don't expect, the usual causes are:

  • A wrong digit in the registration number, often two swapped digits.
  • The wrong postcode: their current one instead of the one on their registration.
  • An old number from an earlier registration or an old email.

Don't keep retrying the same pair. In 2025, registration numbers could be locked for up to 10 minutes after errors or repeated attempts, which would leave that person stuck for the rest of your time. Carry on with the others, ask about that person in the group chat, and come back to them last if there's time.

If you can't fix it before the clock runs out, finish the booking for everyone else. Completing a booking for five is far better than losing all six. Whoever gets through next can try to book the person who was left out.

A registration is locked

In 2025, the official FAQ said registrations could be locked for up to 10 minutes after errors, duplicate attempts or a timeout. If one of your group's registrations is locked, the most likely reasons are that someone else is already booking that person, or that their details were entered wrongly several times. Check the chat before trying again.

The timer runs out

The 2027 sale FAQ said you'd have 10 minutes on the booking page, and tickets aren't allocated until payment has gone through. If the timer ends first, the transaction fails and nothing is booked. Follow the instructions on screen. For the 2025 resale, the advice when a page hung was to press back once, and if that didn't work, to close the browser and start again.

The best protection is speed in the first place: every number and postcode ready to paste, and a card to hand. See using Glasto Quick Copy at the front of the queue.

The card is declined

  • Try your second card. A lead booker paying six deposits is making a bigger payment than usual, and some banks query it.
  • Check your bank's app. Many banks ask you to approve the payment there. If you don't see the request, open the app yourself.
  • Don't be put off by the name. In 2025 the payee could show as "The Way Ahead Group" rather than Glastonbury or See Tickets.
  • Check the card type. For the 2025 resale, American Express wasn't accepted, and international buyers could only use Visa or Mastercard credit cards.
  • Stay on the page. In 2025 the official advice was not to minimise the browser or open new tabs during checkout, as the session could expire.

See paying for Glastonbury tickets for how to get your cards ready beforehand.

"All available tickets have now been allocated"

In 2025 this message meant no new booking pages were available, but tickets from orders that failed could still be released. It's the one time the official advice was to refresh the page. It doesn't mean the sale is over yet.

"SOLD OUT"

This one is final. Every purchase has been processed and no more tickets will be released in that sale. Your next chance is the spring resale.

A plain holding page

Under heavy traffic a reduced, minimal page can appear. In 2025 the festival said this didn't mean the site had crashed. Don't refresh it unless the page tells you to.

No confirmation email

If the confirmation page appeared, screenshot it and the order reference straight away, even before you tell the group. Emails can take a while on a busy morning, so check your spam folder and give it time. If nothing arrives, contact See Tickets customer service with your order reference. Don't try to book the same people again to be safe: you could end up with duplicate bookings or locked registrations.

Two people booked the same friend

This is what an agreed plan of who books whom is for. If it happens anyway, tell the group exactly who's in which booking, and contact See Tickets customer service about the duplicate rather than leaving both in place. See the worked example in buying tickets for a group.

Someone was left out of the booking

Don't count on adding them to the finished booking. If the sale is still going, the next person in your group to get through can book them in a booking of their own. If not, they'll need the resale. Agreeing beforehand what your group does in this situation saves a difficult conversation later.

Most checkout problems come from details that were wrong or hard to find before the sale started. A five minute check of everyone's number and registered postcode the week before prevents most of this page.
