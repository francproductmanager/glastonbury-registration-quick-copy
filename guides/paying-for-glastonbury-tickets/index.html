Glastonbury checkout: paying for tickets and fixing problems

What happens once you're through the queue: the timers, which cards and wallets work, bank checks, and what to do when a name doesn't come up, a registration locks, the timer runs out or a card is declined.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 26 October 2026.

5 min read

Getting to the front of the queue doesn't secure a ticket. Tickets are only allocated once payment has gone through, and there are time limits. This guide is the one place on this site that covers the checkout: how it runs, how to be ready, and what to do when it goes wrong. Where a rule comes from a particular year's official FAQ, we say which.

The clock starts at the front

In the 2025 sale, once you reached the front you had 10 minutes to complete the page where you enter the lead booker's and up to five other people's registration numbers and postcodes, before the session expired. Tickets weren't allocated at that point, only once payment was processed. Reports said that once you reached the payment page, you had five minutes to submit your payment details. The 2027 sale FAQ also gave 10 minutes for the details page. If a timer runs out, the transaction fails and nothing is booked.

That's plenty if everything is ready, and not much if you're scrolling a chat for numbers or looking for your card. The details you enter also can't be changed once you move on, so check them before you continue.

Before the day

  • Know which card you'll use, and that it has enough available for a deposit per person. For 2027 that was £100 a ticket, so £600 for six.
  • Have a second card ready in case the first is declined.
  • Set up Apple Pay or Google Pay in advance if you want to use them. In 2025 they could appear as options depending on your device, but only if they were already set up.
  • Know the payee name. In 2025 the payee could show as "The Way Ahead Group" rather than Glastonbury or See Tickets, for example when paying with Apple Pay or Google Pay. Don't be alarmed by it.
  • Keep your phone to hand for bank security checks, even if you're booking on a laptop. Many banks ask you to approve a payment in their app.

Which cards work, including from outside the UK

For the 2025 resale, UK buyers could use UK debit cards or Visa and Mastercard credit cards. International buyers could only use Visa or Mastercard credit cards, so a non-UK debit card wasn't enough. American Express wasn't accepted from anyone. Check the official FAQ for each sale, as this can change.

If you're buying from abroad, a few more things are worth sorting before the day: prices are in pounds, so check your card's limit and foreign transaction fees against a deposit for everyone you're booking; tell your bank to expect a large payment to a UK company; and work out what time the sale opens where you are, since sale times are UK time.

During checkout

  1. Paste each registration number and registered postcode. Check the names that come back are the people you expect.
  2. Confirm or amend the billing address.
  3. Enter payment details, or use Apple Pay or Google Pay.
  4. Approve the payment in your bank's app if asked.
  5. Accept the terms and complete the order before the timer ends.
  6. Screenshot the confirmation page and the order reference, then tell your group.

Don't minimise the browser or open new tabs during checkout: the official advice in 2025 was that the session could expire if you did.

When it goes wrong

Whatever goes wrong, post one short message in the group straight away, like "FAILED, still need Group A". Someone else may still be in the queue, and they need to know those people aren't covered yet. Then work through the problem below.

A friend's name doesn't come up

The usual causes are a wrong digit (often two swapped), their current postcode instead of the registered one, or an old number from an earlier registration. Don't keep retrying the same pair: in the 2025 resale, registration numbers could be locked for up to 10 minutes after errors or repeated attempts. Carry on with the others, ask about that person in the chat, and come back to them last if there's time.

If you can't fix it before the clock runs out, finish the booking for everyone else. A booking for five is far better than losing all six, and whoever gets through next can try to book the person who was left out.

A registration is locked

The festival's 2025 resale FAQ said registrations could be locked for up to 10 minutes after errors, duplicate attempts or a timeout. The most likely reasons are that someone else is already booking that person, or that their details were entered wrongly several times. Check the chat before trying again.

The timer runs out

The transaction fails and nothing is booked. Follow the instructions on screen. For the 2025 resale, the advice when a page hung was to press back once, and if that didn't work, to close the browser and start again. The best protection is speed in the first place: see using Glasto Quick Copy on ticket day.

The card is declined

  • Try your second card. Six deposits is a bigger payment than usual, and some banks query it.
  • Check your bank's app. If you don't see an approval request, open the app yourself.
  • Check the card type. American Express wasn't accepted in 2025.
  • Stay on the page while you sort it out.

Messages you might see

  • "All available tickets have now been allocated". In 2025 this meant no new booking pages were available, but tickets from failed orders could still be released. It's the one time the official advice was to refresh the page.
  • "SOLD OUT". All purchases have been processed and no more tickets will be released in that sale.
  • A plain holding page. In 2025 the festival said this didn't mean the site had crashed.

No confirmation email

If the confirmation page appeared, you should have the screenshot and order reference. Emails can take a while on a busy morning, so check your spam folder and give it time. If nothing arrives, contact See Tickets customer service with your order reference. Don't book the same people again to be safe: you could end up with duplicate bookings or locked registrations.

Two people booked the same friend

This is what an agreed plan of who books whom is for. If it happens anyway, tell the group exactly who's in which booking, and contact See Tickets customer service about the duplicate rather than leaving both in place.

Someone was left out

Don't count on adding them to the finished booking. If the sale is still going, the next person in your group to get through can book them in a booking of their own. If not, they'll need the resale.

In the resale

Resale tickets are paid in full at the time of booking, not with a deposit. That's a much bigger payment for a group: for four people at the 2027 price it would be over £1,600. Check your card limit beforehand and tell your bank if it usually flags large payments. See the 2027 resale.
