How to register for Glastonbury: photo, number and deadlines

Who needs to register, how to do it step by step, what makes a photo pass, when registration reopens, and how to find or change your details.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 29 October 2026.

5 min read

You can't buy a Glastonbury ticket for anyone who hasn't registered first. Registration links a photo to each person, and that photo is printed on their ticket. It's how the festival stops touts: tickets are non-transferable, and security checks that the person at the gate matches the photo.

For a group, registration is where most ticket day problems actually start. Someone registered years ago with an old email, someone's photo was rejected and they never noticed, someone moved house. This guide covers how registration works and how to check your whole group is ready, well before the sale.

Who needs to register

Everyone who will be 13 or over on the day the gates open needs a ticket, and so needs to be registered. Children aged 12 and under don't need a ticket and don't register.

Watch the birthday rule if you have teenagers in the group: it's their age on the day the gates open that counts, not their age on ticket day. A 12 year old who turns 13 before the festival needs registering.

If you're buying for friends, each of them must have their own registration. On ticket day you'll need two things for every person: their registration number and the postcode they registered with.

What you need to register

  • A few basic contact details, including an email address you check. Use one you'll still have next year: it's how you get your number back if you lose it.
  • A recent, passport-style photo of your face: facing the camera, well lit, no sunglasses or hat, and only you in the picture.
  • A few minutes. Registration is free and done on the official See Tickets registration page.

How to register, step by step

  1. Go to the official registration page on glastonbury.seetickets.com. Start from the festival's own website if you want to be sure you're in the right place: registration is never done anywhere else.
  2. Fill in your details. Use an email address you'll still have next year, and the postcode you'll give your group, because that's the one the lead booker will type.
  3. Upload your photo (see below for what passes).
  4. Submit, and wait for the photo check.
  5. Open the email that follows. It confirms the photo was accepted and gives your registration number. Forward it to whoever's organising your group.

Getting the photo right first time

The photo is checked by a person, so the aim is simply a clear, honest picture of your face. Most rejections come from a handful of avoidable things:

  • Cropped from a group or night out photo, with part of someone else's face or shoulder still in shot.
  • Sunglasses, a cap or a hood, or heavy shadow across your eyes.
  • Filters and effects from social apps, or a heavily edited selfie.
  • Too dark or too far away, so your face is a small part of the picture.
  • A photo that no longer looks like you, like one from years ago with very different hair or a beard you've since lost.

The easiest approach is to stand facing a window in daylight, against a plain wall, and have someone take the picture at head height.

How long the photo check takes

After you submit, your photo is checked by hand. The festival says this happens within about 48 hours in the working week. If it's accepted, you get an email with your registration number and registered postcode.

If your photo is rejected, you'll get an email telling you. If you haven't heard anything within a further 48 hours, the advice is to register again.

Register weeks before the sale, not days. Registration is usually paused around ticket sales, and you may need time to resubmit a photo.

Keeping your photo up to date

Your confirmation email includes links to view, edit or delete your registration and to update your photo. The festival encourages updating the photo every year so it still looks like you.

You can't change your photo while you have a booking linked to your registration, so update it before ticket day if it's out of date. The official advice is to check your registration at least 7 days before registration closes.

Moved house since you registered?

On ticket day the lead booker enters the postcode that's on your registration, not your current one. If you've moved, either tell the lead booker your old registered postcode, or update your registration before the sale so the new one is on record. What matters is that the postcode they type matches the registration exactly.

When does registration reopen?

Registration is paused around each sale. For 2027, it closed at 5pm on Friday 25 September 2026, and the festival said it would not reopen until after the October ticket sales. It now says registration is also suspended before and during the sale for local residents on Sunday 1 November 2026, and will reopen later in the autumn. The exact date hasn't been announced yet. We add it to the 2027 key dates as soon as it is.

If you already have a registration, you don't need a new one for the resale or for the next festival: it carries over. You only need to check that the photo still looks like you and that the details are right while registration is open.

Lost your number, or need to change something?

The official registration lookup on See Tickets sends your registration number to the email address you registered with, along with links to update your photo and details. What to do if it finds nothing, and how to change your photo, postcode or email, is in finding or changing your registration.

A five minute check for every friend

If you're the one organising, ask each person to forward you their latest confirmation email, or send you a screenshot of it. Then check four things:

  1. The photo was accepted, not just submitted.
  2. The registration number is the one in their most recent email, in case they registered twice.
  3. The postcode is the one on the registration, even if they've moved.
  4. The name is spelled the way you'd recognise it, because the booking page shows names back to you and you'll want to spot a wrong one fast.

Keep a simple list as you go: who's confirmed, who's waiting on a photo check, who hasn't registered yet. Chase the last group early, because the photo check alone can take a couple of working days.

Getting your group's numbers together

Ask everyone in your group to send you their registration number and registered postcode well before the sale. Check each one against the confirmation email if you can: a single wrong digit means that person won't be added to your booking.
