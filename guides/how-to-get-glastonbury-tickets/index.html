How to get Glastonbury tickets: giving your group the best chance

What really improves your chances under the queue used for the 2027 sale, what doesn't, and a simple plan for a group across the coach sale, the general sale and the resale.

By the maker of Glasto Quick Copy (about us). Facts checked 26 October 2026.

There's no trick that gets you to the front of the Glastonbury queue. Demand is many times the number of tickets, and every recent sale has sold out in under an hour. But the groups that get tickets tend to have done the same few things, and the groups that miss out often lose a real chance to something they could have fixed a week earlier. This guide puts those things in one place, based on how the 2027 sale worked.

What changed in 2027

For the 2027 sales, buyers passed a security check and then entered their registration details before joining the queue. Each registration got one place. Reports from the sale said a second phone, another browser or a different connection with the same registration number brought up the same place, not a new one.

The festival's advice was to be in the waiting room at glastonbury.seetickets.com at least 10 minutes before the sale, because anyone arriving after the start went to the back. Not refreshing, and not using several tabs or devices, was part of the official advice too.

Time Out noted that the old approach, where friends each ran as many devices as they could, was likely to be far less successful under the new system. Buyers who did get through said it felt fairer than ever.

The one thing that really raises your odds

If each registration gets one place, then the number of chances your group has is simply the number of people with their own registration who queue. Six friends queueing on one device each have six places. One person with six devices has one.

Whoever gets through first can book up to six people, so every extra person queueing is an extra chance for the whole group, not just for themselves. That changes what's worth doing weeks before the sale:

  • Get everyone going registered, including the people who'd normally leave it to the organiser. A friend who "doesn't need to do anything" because someone else is booking is a chance your group isn't using.
  • Ask everyone who's free at sale time to queue. Partners, siblings and the friend who's never bothered before all count, as long as they're coming and registered.
  • Give every queuer the group's details, so whoever gets through can book everyone without asking for anything.

Use every sale, not just one

Each sale is a separate chance. These were the 2027 dates and results:

Many groups try the coach sale first, then general admission if it doesn't work, then the resale. Agree the rules beforehand: a coach package means travelling on a booked coach from one departure town, and if some of you get coach tickets, nobody should then book the same people in the general sale. Don't waste a place once you get one

Getting through the queue is luck. What happens next isn't. The 2027 sale FAQ said you'd have 10 minutes on the booking page, and tickets aren't allocated until payment goes through. Places are lost at this stage to things that are all fixable in advance:

  • Numbers that aren't ready to paste. Scrolling a chat for six ten digit numbers is slow and error prone. Put them on one page with Glasto Quick Copy.
  • The wrong postcode. It must be the one on the registration, not where someone lives now.
  • A card that's declined. A deposit for six was £600 in 2027. Check the limit and have a second card ready. See paying for Glastonbury tickets.
  • No plan for who books whom. Two people booking the same friends wastes a turn. See buying tickets for a group.
  • A phone that locks or dies. See setting up your phone or laptop.

What doesn't help

  • Extra tabs and devices on the same registration. They gave the same place in 2027, and the official advice was against them.
  • Refreshing. A slow queue page is normal. The festival warned that refreshing could lose your place or disrupt your booking.
  • VPNs. Some buyers in 2027 reported "access restricted" messages. Use your normal connection.
  • Arriving at the last second. Be in the waiting room at least 10 minutes early. Arriving even earlier than that didn't move you forward in 2027.
  • Paid services, bots and "guaranteed" access. Nobody can sell you a better place in the queue, and handing your registration to strangers puts it at risk. It's also against the sale's rules. See how to spot Glastonbury ticket scams.

A plan for your group

  1. Four or more weeks before: everyone who's going registers, or checks their registration and photo are up to date.
  2. Two weeks before: collect every registration number and registered postcode in one place, and send everyone the group's page.
  3. A week before: agree who books whom, especially if you're more than six. Agree how deposits will be paid back.
  4. The night before: set up your phones and laptops, check your cards, and open the group's page once to check it works.
  5. Sale time: everyone who's free is in the waiting room at least 10 minutes early, on one device each, with their own registration.
  6. When someone gets through: they post in the chat, book everyone they've agreed to cover, and post again when it's confirmed.

If it doesn't work this time

Most people who try don't get tickets, so plan for that too. Agree now whether your group will try the resale together, keep everyone's registration up to date, and keep the group's details together. See other ways to get to Glastonbury.

Count your chances: the number of people in your group who are registered, going and free at sale time. Raising that number is the one thing that changes your odds.
