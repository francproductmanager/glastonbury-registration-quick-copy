Buying Glastonbury tickets for a group

How one lead booker buys for up to six people, what they need from everyone, a worked plan for a bigger group, and how to split the cost.

By the maker of Glasto Quick Copy (about us). Facts checked 20 October 2026.

Most people go to Glastonbury with friends, and the booking system is built for that. One person can buy tickets for a small group in one go, as long as they have everyone's details. The hard part is usually not the booking itself but the organising around it.

Up to six people per booking

A lead booker can buy up to six tickets in one transaction: their own and up to five more. For each person they enter that person's registration number and registered postcode.

The lead booker pays the deposit for everyone in the booking. Everyone in the booking must be registered with an accepted photo before the sale.

What to collect from each friend

  • Their registration number, copied from their confirmation email.
  • The postcode they registered with. If they've moved since, it's still the registered postcode that matters, unless they've updated their registration.
  • Confirmation that their photo was accepted.
  • Whether they'll be free to queue on the day, so you know how many chances your group has.

Ask for the details as text, not a photo of the email, so they can be copied rather than retyped.

Groups bigger than six

Split into booking groups of up to six and give each group more than one person who'll try in the queue. Write down who covers whom. When a booking goes through, cross those people off straight away so nobody is booked twice and nobody is forgotten.

A worked example: nine friends

Say nine friends want to go together. They split into two booking groups:

  • Group A, six people: the six who most want to camp together.
  • Group B, three people: the other three.

Everyone queues. The rule they agree is simple: the first person through books Group A; the second person through books Group B. It doesn't matter which group the bookers themselves are in. If the first person through happens to be in Group B, they still book Group A first, then post in the chat so the next person through knows to book Group B.

Each booker has a page with both groups on it. With Glasto Quick Copy that can be two saved groups, so the booker just opens the right one.

This avoids the two most common big group failures: two people booking the same six friends, and three people being forgotten because everyone assumed someone else had them.

Couples and friends who must go together

If two people won't go without each other, put them in the same booking group. Separate bookings can succeed or fail independently, so splitting a couple across groups risks one going without the other.

Sharing the numbers safely

Registration numbers and postcodes aren't passwords, but they are personal details. Share them only with your group, not in public posts. With Glasto Quick Copy the lead booker can put everyone on one page and send the group a link that only contains first names, numbers and postcodes.

Paying each other back

Agree before the day how deposits and balances will be split. A short message in the group chat with the amounts and dates saves awkward conversations later.

Using the 2027 deposit as an example: a lead booker buying for six paid £100 per ticket, so £600 on the day. Each of the other five then owes them £100. Settle the deposit within a few days while everyone's excited, and agree separately how the balance in the spring will be handled, so nobody is out of pocket for months.

Ask everyone to send their share before ticket day if your group trusts each other. The lead booker then isn't chasing anyone afterwards. If someone doesn't get a ticket, they get their money straight back.

If only some of you get tickets

It happens a lot: one booking group gets through and the other doesn't. Talk about this before the day, not after. Some groups agree to all try the resale together; others agree that whoever gets a ticket goes either way. Either is fine, as long as it's agreed beforehand.
