How to split Glastonbury ticket costs fairly in a group

Splitting Glastonbury 2027 ticket costs in a group: paying back the lead booker, balances together or separately, a worked example for six, and dropouts.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 20 November 2026.

5 min read

On ticket day one person, the lead booker, pays every deposit in the booking, so the rest of the group needs to pay them back. In April each balance can be paid by the lead booker for everyone, or by each person from their own card. Paying together saves postage; paying separately means each person gets their own ticket and settles their own share. Either works, as long as the group agrees which before the balance window and writes it down.

The rules here are from the payment, dispatch and cancellation sections of the festival's information page, read in full on 20 November 2026. The deposit and balance themselves are explained in Glastonbury deposits and balance payments.

Who pays what, and when

WhenWho paysHow much a person (2027)
Ticket dayThe lead booker, for everyone in the bookingThe £100 deposit (on a ticket and coach package, plus the coach fare and a £3 coach administration fee)
Balance window, 1 to 7 April 2027The lead booker for everyone, or each person for themselvesThe £308 balance, plus postage
With a balanceWhoever pays that balanceOptional extras: a car park pass and Refund Protection

The festival says plainly that each person must make sure their own balance is paid. So even if the lead booker pays, everyone should check their ticket has been.

Paying the balance together or separately

The festival says that if a deposit was paid for more than one ticket, the balances can be paid together or individually, and from other cards. What changes is the postage and where the tickets go:

Paid together by one personEach person pays their own
PostageOne £11.25 charge for the whole order£11.25 for each ticket, because each one becomes a booking of its own
Where the tickets are sentAll to the address given for the first name on the bookingEach person receives their own ticket
Who pays back whomEveryone pays back the person who paidNobody needs paying back for the balance

Car park passes are bought with a balance, and only once at least one balance on the booking has been paid. The festival lists a pass at £60, plus £3 postage where bought separately, for general admission only. Decide who in each car buys it, and split it between the people in that car.

A worked example: six friends

Six friends get one booking of six general admission tickets in the 2027 sale.

On ticket day

The lead booker pays six deposits, £600 in all. Each of the other five then pays them back £100, ideally within a few days, while the good news is fresh.

In April, option 1: the lead booker pays every balance

Amount
Six balances£1,848
One postage charge£11.25
Paid by the lead booker£1,859.25
Each person's share£309.88 (the balance plus a sixth of the postage, rounded to the penny)

Each of the other five pays the lead booker back £309.88, and all six tickets are sent to the address given for the first name on the booking.

In April, option 2: everyone pays their own

Amount
Each person pays See Tickets£319.25 (their balance plus their own postage)
Postage for the group£67.50, six charges instead of one
Extra cost of paying separately£56.25 for the group, or £9.38 each

The lead booker isn't out of pocket for anyone's balance, and each ticket goes to its own holder.

Which way suits your group?

  • Pay together if one person is happy to pay over £1,800 in one go and wait to be paid back, and everyone can collect their ticket from them before the festival.
  • Pay separately if you live far apart, or if nobody wants to cover everyone's balance. It costs the group more in postage, but it keeps each person's money and ticket their own.

If someone drops out

Tickets carry the holder's photo and can't be transferred, so a dropout can't pass their place to a friend. What happens to the money depends on when they drop out:

  • Before the balance is paid. An unpaid balance cancels that ticket, and its deposit, less a £25 charge, goes back to the card it was booked on: the lead booker's. So the lead booker gets £75 back, which belongs to whoever paid them that deposit.
  • After the balance is paid. The ticket holder asks See Tickets to cancel before 7 May 2027. The same £25 charge applies, plus a £15 coach cancellation fee on a ticket and coach booking.
  • Refund Protection is bought per person when paying the balance. If one person pays everyone's balance, ask each person first whether they want it for their ticket.

The festival doesn't say which card the money for a paid ticket goes back to, so agree in advance that it ends up with whoever paid for that ticket. The full rules and deadlines are in Glastonbury ticket refunds. A friend who wants the place can try the spring resale, which is paid in full at booking.

Settling up: one message for the group chat

Put the plan in writing once, straight after booking, so nobody is guessing in April. For option 1 it could be as simple as:

  • Ticket day: six deposits on the lead booker's card. Everyone else pays them back £100 this week.
  • April: the lead booker pays all six balances, 1 to 7 April 2027. Everyone pays them £309.88 before then.
  • Car park pass: bought with the balance, split between the people in the car.
  • Can't go? Say so before the balance is paid.

More ready-made messages are in messages to send your Glastonbury group, and the group spreadsheet template has a column for whether each deposit has been paid back.

What we couldn't confirm

The information page doesn't say which card the money for a cancelled, fully paid ticket goes back to, or whether one booking can have some balances paid together and others separately. If either matters to your group, ask See Tickets before the balance window.
