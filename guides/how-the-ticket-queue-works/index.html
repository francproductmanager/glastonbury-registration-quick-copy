How the Glastonbury ticket queue works, and what its errors mean

What happens when the sale opens, why extra tabs no longer help, what the green blocks, "access restricted" and the holding page mean, and how a group should queue.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 1 November 2026.

5 min read

Demand for Glastonbury tickets is many times the number available, so the sale runs through an online waiting room. This guide is the one place on this site that explains how the queue works: joining it, what each screen means, what to do when something looks wrong, and how a group can use it sensibly.

Joining the queue

In the 2027 sale, Time Out reported, buyers had to complete a captcha and then enter their registration number before joining the queue. Each person was given a unique queue ID linked to their registration, so each person could only enter the queue once.

When the sale opens, everyone already waiting is given a place in the queue at random, and anyone arriving later joins the back. So be early, but there's no prize for being very early: the festival's information page says to be online at glastonbury.seetickets.com at least 10 minutes before the sale opens.

Because the queue is tied to registrations, Time Out reported that it was no longer possible to queue on several tabs or devices. The festival's information page goes further: don't refresh the page or use several devices or tabs, because you will lose your place in the queue. Several people in your group can each try with their own registration, though, and whoever gets through first books for everyone.

What the queue screen shows

In 2027 progress was shown as rows of blocks that turned green as you moved forward, not as a numbered position. You don't get told how many people are ahead of you, and the blocks can sit unchanged for a long time. NME reported that plenty of fans found that frustrating. It's normal, and it doesn't mean you've been forgotten.

If your connection drops, don't start several new attempts. Your place is linked to your registration, so open the queue again once, on the same device, and follow what the page says.

What you can and can't control

It helps to be clear about this, because ticket morning is stressful and it's easy to waste energy on the wrong things.

  • You can't control where you land in the queue, how fast it moves or whether tickets last until you reach the front.
  • You can control whether everyone is registered, whether the details are ready to paste, whether your device stays awake, and whether your group knows who's booking for whom.

Everything in the second list can be done days before. That's where the time is best spent.

How a group should queue

Every person in your group who has their own registration can queue on their own device. More people queueing means more chances that someone gets through. The catch is that once someone does, everyone else needs to know.

  1. Everyone who's free queues with their own registration number, on one device each.
  2. Everyone has the same page of the group's details, so whoever gets through can book without asking for anything.
  3. The first person through posts one message: "I'm in, booking all six."
  4. Everyone else keeps their place but doesn't book until they hear whether it worked.
  5. When the booking is confirmed, the booker posts who's covered. If the group is bigger than six, the next person through books the rest.

Why this matters so much under the current queue is explained in Glastonbury ticket tips, and a full plan for bigger groups is in the syndicate guide.

While you wait

  • Leave the page open and let it move on by itself.
  • Use your normal connection. The 2025 sale FAQ warned that refreshing the page, using multiple tabs or many devices can look like suspicious behaviour and trigger the anti-bot software.
  • Keep the device awake and plugged in. See phone or laptop: setting up either.
  • Have your group's numbers ready to paste the moment you get through.
  • Keep the group chat quiet. A stream of "still waiting" messages makes the one that matters easy to miss.

At the front

Reaching the front opens the booking page, with a timer. Tickets aren't allocated until payment goes through, so the queue is only half the job. The timers, the cards that work and what to do when something fails are in the checkout guide.

If someone in your group gets through

They should book for everyone they've agreed to cover, up to six people. As soon as the booking is confirmed, they should tell the group, so nobody else spends their turn booking the same people. If a booking fails part way through, they should say so straight away too. Someone else further back in the queue may still be able to book.

Myths worth ignoring

  • "Open as many tabs as you can." Not since the queue was linked to registration numbers. The festival says using several devices or tabs will lose you your place.
  • "Refresh when it looks stuck." A slow page is normal. Refreshing is more likely to cost you than help.
  • "Join an hour early to be first." Everyone in the waiting room at the start gets a random place.
  • "Someone on social media has a trick." If it involves buying, sharing your registration or using another site, it's not official and could be a scam.

The queue can't be beaten, but the minute after it can be slowed down by missing numbers. Have them ready.
