How the Glastonbury ticket queue works

What happens when the sale opens, why extra tabs no longer help, how a group should split up in the queue and what you can and can't control.

By the maker of Glasto Quick Copy (about us). Facts checked 20 October 2026.

Demand for Glastonbury tickets is many times the number available, so the sale runs through an online waiting room. Here's how it has worked in recent years, what that means for you, and how a group can use it sensibly.

Joining the queue

In the 2027 sale, buyers passed a security check and then had to enter their registration details before joining the queue. One registration meant one place in the queue, and progress was shown as rows of blocks that turned green as you moved forward, rather than a numbered position.

In earlier sales, everyone already on the page when the sale opened was placed in the queue at random, and anyone arriving later joined the back. Either way, being on the page early matters: the 2027 sale FAQ said to be in the waiting room at least 10 minutes before the start. Refreshing doesn't help.

Linking the queue to registrations means opening more tabs, browsers or devices with the same registration number gives you the same place, not a second chance. Several people in your group can each try with their own registration number, though, and whoever gets through first books for everyone.

What you can and can't control

It helps to be clear about this, because ticket morning is stressful and it's easy to waste energy on the wrong things.

  • You can't control where you land in the queue, how fast it moves or whether tickets last until you reach the front.
  • You can control whether everyone is registered, whether the details are ready to paste, whether your device stays awake, and whether your group knows who's booking for whom.

Everything in the second list can be done days before. That's where the time is best spent.

How a group should queue

Every person in your group who has their own registration can queue on their own device. More people queueing means more chances that someone gets through. The catch is that once someone does, everyone else needs to know.

A simple plan that works for most groups:

  1. Everyone who's free queues with their own registration number, on one device each.
  2. Everyone has the same page of the group's details, so whoever gets through can book without asking for anything.
  3. The first person through posts one message: "I'm in, booking all six."
  4. Everyone else keeps their place but doesn't book until they hear whether it worked.
  5. When the booking is confirmed, the booker posts who's covered. If the group is bigger than six, the next person through books the rest.

Why this matters so much under the current queue is explained in how to get Glastonbury tickets.

While you wait

  • Leave the page open and let it move on by itself.
  • Don't refresh unless the official instructions say to. Refreshing can lose your place or trip the anti-bot checks.
  • Don't use a VPN. Some buyers in 2027 reported "access restricted" messages, and the anti-bot checks look for unusual connections.
  • Keep the device awake and plugged in.
  • Have your group's numbers ready to paste the moment you get through.
  • Keep the group chat quiet. A stream of "still waiting" messages makes the one that matters easy to miss.

At the front: the 10 minute clock

In 2025, reaching the front gave you 10 minutes to enter the registration numbers and postcodes, pay and finish. Tickets weren't allocated until payment went through, and details couldn't be changed once you moved on. That's why having everyone's details ready to paste matters: it turns the most stressful part of the morning into a minute's work. See paying for Glastonbury tickets.

If someone in your group gets through

They should book for everyone they've agreed to cover, up to six people. As soon as the booking is confirmed, they should tell the group, so nobody else spends their turn booking the same people.

If a booking fails part way through, they should say so straight away too. Someone else further back in the queue may still be able to book.

If you don't get a ticket

There's usually a second chance in the spring resale, when tickets that weren't fully paid for are sold again. See our resale guide. It's worth keeping everyone's registration and the group's details ready for it.

Myths worth ignoring

  • "Open as many tabs as you can." Not since the queue was linked to registration numbers. It just slows your device.
  • "Refresh when it looks stuck." A slow page is normal. Refreshing is more likely to cost you than help.
  • "Someone on social media has a trick." If it involves buying, sharing your registration or using another site, it's not official and could be a scam.

The queue can't be beaten, but the minute after it can be slowed down by missing numbers. Have them ready.
