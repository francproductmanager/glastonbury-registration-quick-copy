Are there Glastonbury VIP tickets? Hospitality and backstage explained

There are no VIP or backstage tickets for Glastonbury. What hospitality tickets really are, who can buy them, and how to spot a fake VIP or concierge offer.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 10 November 2026.

5 min read

No. There is no such thing as a VIP ticket, VIP pass or backstage pass for Glastonbury. The festival says so in plain words on its information page, and adds that anyone claiming to be able to provide one is bogus. What does exist is a small number of hospitality tickets, sold in two narrow ways. This guide explains both, and how to tell a genuine offer from a scam.

What the festival says about VIP tickets

The festival's information page, in its anti-touting guide, has a section headed "VIP tickets/passes". It says: "For the avoidance of doubt, there is no such thing as a VIP ticket/pass/backstage pass for Glastonbury. Anyone claiming to be able to provide such a thing is bogus."

The same page repeats it in capitals in its ticket section: there are no "VIP" or "backstage" tickets available to buy for Glastonbury Festival. So if you see a listing, an advert or a message offering a VIP package or a backstage upgrade, you don't need to judge whether the seller seems trustworthy. The thing they're selling doesn't exist.

What hospitality tickets are

Hospitality tickets are real, but they aren't a premium version of the general sale that anyone can buy. The festival's information page says:

  • They're for the media and people working in the music industry (and related media), not the general public.
  • You apply for them. They're sold on an application basis, directly by the festival.
  • The festival is the only authorised seller of stand alone hospitality tickets. It doesn't let any other vendor or agency sell them on their own.
  • For 2027, applications open in early spring 2027. The page gives no exact date.
  • The festival's rules still apply. Hospitality tickets are subject to its photography policy and its broadcaster, internet and filming policy, as general admission tickets are.
  • You need original ID. The page's list of what to bring says original ID documents, not photocopies, are needed for hospitality tickets.

The information page doesn't give a price for hospitality tickets, and it doesn't say what they include. For anything beyond the points above, it says to contact the festival's hospitality team.

Hospitality as part of a neighbour's accommodation

There is one other legitimate route. A handful of the festival's neighbours offer hospitality tickets strictly as part of the accommodation on their own property. The festival sets limits on this too:

  • These tickets can't be booked through third parties or agencies.
  • They can't be sold on a ticket only basis. They come with the neighbour's accommodation, not on their own.
  • If you have any doubt about whether a neighbouring hospitality provider is genuine, the festival asks you to contact it to check.

So an offer of "hospitality tickets, no accommodation needed", or one made by an agency on a neighbour's behalf, doesn't match how the festival says these work.

Concierge services and third party sellers

The festival says it doesn't supply tickets to any "concierge services". In the accommodation section of the same page it warns that people keep falling victim to third party concierge services that claim they can source tickets, often above face value, and then fail to deliver. It asks anyone offered tickets by a third party to contact it.

How to check an offer

Here is each kind of offer you might see, set against what the festival's information page says:

The offerWhat the festival says
A VIP ticket or VIP passThere is no such thing. Anyone offering one is bogus.
A backstage pass or backstage ticketThere is no such thing to buy. Anyone offering one is bogus.
Hospitality tickets from an agency or ticket siteOnly the festival sells stand alone hospitality tickets. No other vendor or agency may.
Hospitality tickets from a neighbour, without accommodationNot allowed: neighbours may only offer them as part of accommodation on their property.
Tickets through a concierge serviceThe festival doesn't supply tickets to any concierge service.
A print at home ticket or e-ticket for entryThe festival doesn't issue them for entry. Any offered for sale are a scam.
A ticket on an auction or resale siteThe festival doesn't supply tickets to them. Cancelled tickets are resold at face value only in the official See Tickets resale.

If an offer isn't on that list but still feels like a shortcut, the festival's general rule is simple: the only authorised seller of general admission and Sunday tickets is See Tickets, and a ticket offered by anyone else is not likely to be legitimate and won't get you in. The festival also says it can't help you if you bought from an unauthorised source. The wider list of ticket scams, and what to do if you've already paid, is in how to spot Glastonbury ticket scams.

Who does get backstage?

Backstage and production areas are part of the festival site, but with no backstage ticket on sale, nobody can buy their way in. The festival's filming policy names backstage, production and artist hospitality areas among the places where recording and photography are banned. For a sense of who else is on site, the festival's 2010 history page gives that year's attendance as 135,000 weekend tickets, 5,000 Sunday tickets and 37,500 passes for crew, performers, stewards and traders. Those are 2010 figures, not this year's. If working at the festival appeals, see volunteering at Glastonbury 2027 and other ways to get to Glastonbury.

Want a more comfortable Glastonbury?

What people often mean by VIP is somewhere nicer to sleep. That part is real, and the festival sells it itself:

  • Worthy View, tipis and other accommodation, booked through the festival once it opens. You must already have a valid ticket booking to book accommodation.
  • Campervan and caravan pitches, sold in their own sale for general admission ticket holders.
  • Rooms and houses near the site, which a limited number of local property owners rent out. The festival's information page asks people interested to contact it through its contact form.

Prices, rules and, once announced, sale dates are in Glastonbury 2027 campervans, Worthy View and tipis. If you have access requirements, see Glastonbury 2027 accessibility.

If you've already paid for a VIP ticket

If you've tried to buy tickets through a seller who may not be genuine, the festival's information page says to contact your payment card issuer as soon as possible. If you believe there is criminality involved, it says to contact the police, along with advice from your card issuer. The same section asks anyone with concerns about a person or company claiming to have Glastonbury tickets to contact the festival to verify the details, and says you can also report it to Action Fraud. More on what to do next is in how to spot Glastonbury ticket scams.

In short

  • There are no VIP or backstage tickets for Glastonbury, at any price.
  • Hospitality tickets exist, but only for media and music industry applicants, sold by the festival, or as part of a neighbour's accommodation.
  • Nobody else, no agency and no concierge service, can sell you either kind.
  • For a standard ticket, the only seller is See Tickets: see how to get Glastonbury tickets.
