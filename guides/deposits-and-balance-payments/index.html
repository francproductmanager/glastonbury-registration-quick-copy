Glastonbury deposits and balance payments explained

Why you pay a deposit first, when the balance is due, a worked cost example, and what happens if you can't pay or can't go.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 26 October 2026.

3 min read

Glastonbury general admission tickets are bought in two parts: a deposit when you book, and the balance in the spring. This spreads the cost, but it also means there's a deadline to remember, and in a group, money to sort out between friends.

The deposit

When you book, the lead booker pays a deposit for each person in the booking. In the 2027 sale the deposit was £100 per ticket, on a ticket price of £408 including a £5 booking fee. For ticket and coach packages, the coach fare is added to the deposit. In 2025 the deposit was £75.

The balance

The rest is paid during a set balance payment window, usually in the first week of April. For 2027 it runs from 9am on Thursday 1 April to 11.59pm on Wednesday 7 April 2027, UK time. Check the official balance payment guidance each year for exactly who can pay and how. The festival and See Tickets send reminders, but it's your responsibility to pay in time.

Parking, protection and the rest of that week are covered in the 2027 balance payment guide.

A worked example

Using the 2027 prices, here's how the cost breaks down for one ticket. The £5 booking fee is already included in the £408.

  • Ticket price: £408
  • Deposit on ticket day: £100
  • Balance in the spring: £408 minus £100, so £308
  • Postage and packing: £11.25 per order, paid with the balance

For a lead booker buying six tickets, that's £600 on ticket day, then six balances of £308 and one postage charge in the spring: £1,859.25 in the April window for the whole booking.

With the 2027 sale in October and the balance due in early April, that's about six months. Putting aside around £52 a month covers a £308 balance. It's a small habit that saves a stressful week in March.

Who pays what in a group

However your group handles it, write it down once in the group chat after booking: who paid the deposits, who owes whom, and the balance dates. A message like this is enough:

  • Deposits paid by the lead booker: £600 for six.
  • Everyone else owes the lead booker £100 by the end of the week.
  • Balance of £308 each, due in the April window. Reminder set.

If the balance isn't paid

If a balance isn't paid by the end of the window, that ticket is cancelled and goes back on sale in the spring resale. In 2025 the deposit was refunded automatically to the card it was paid with, minus an administration fee: £50 back from a £75 deposit, within about seven working days of the window closing. If the card had changed, people had to contact See Tickets to arrange the refund.

This is the most painful mistake on this page, because it happens months after the hard part. A card that's expired since ticket day, a missed email or a holiday in the payment week are the usual causes. Calendar reminders for everyone in the group, not just the lead booker, guard against all three.

If you can't go

Tickets can't be transferred to anyone else. For 2027, general admission tickets are refundable until 11.59pm on 7 May 2027, minus a £25 administration charge per ticket, and not refundable after that. The festival's ticket terms say booking fees, postage and packing are not refunded. The full rules are in Glastonbury ticket refunds.

If someone in your group drops out, they can't hand their ticket to a friend. The friend's best route is the resale, with their own registration.

Add the balance week to your calendar

Friends still without a ticket? The spring resale is paid in full at booking, up to four people per booking in 2025: get their resale group ready.
