Glastonbury ticket day checklist

A printable checklist for two weeks before, the night before and the minutes before the Glastonbury sale, who does what, and the ten mistakes to avoid.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 30 October 2026.

5 min read

Glastonbury tickets usually sell out in under an hour. The queue is luck, but what happens after you get through isn't. This checklist covers the things you can control, in the order you'll need them. Tick the boxes as you go, or print it: nothing you tick is saved.

Two weeks or more before

Give everyone a job

Groups that get organised tend to split ticket morning into three simple roles. In a small group one person can do more than one.

  • Queuers. Everyone with their own registration who's free at the time. Each queues on their own device with their own registration number, and each has the group's details ready to paste.
  • The payer. Whoever gets through pays the deposits. If that might not be the same person each time, make sure every queuer has a card that can cover the group.
  • The coordinator. One person keeps the group chat tidy: they confirm when a booking is done and who it covered, so everyone else knows to stop or carry on.

When you get through

  1. Tell the group straight away with one short message, so nobody books the same people.
  2. Enter the lead booker's details, then paste each person's registration number and registered postcode.
  3. Check the names that appear match your group before you continue.
  4. Pay the deposits and wait for the confirmation page.
  5. Screenshot the confirmation and the order reference, and tell the group who's booked.

There's a timer on the booking page. If a name doesn't come up, don't keep retrying the same pair: carry on with the others and come back to that person last. Everything else that can go wrong at checkout is in the checkout guide.

Copy each number with one tap instead of typing it. Typing six ten digit numbers under pressure is where mistakes happen.

After you've booked

The ten mistakes to avoid

Most ticket day disappointment is down to the queue. But every year some groups get through and still lose time or places to avoidable slips. Almost all of them can be fixed days before the sale.

MistakeHow to avoid it
Someone isn't registered, or their photo was rejectedAsk everyone to forward their latest confirmation email and check it says the photo was accepted. See registration.
The wrong postcodeAsk "which postcode is on your registration?", not "what's your postcode?". Students and people who've moved are the usual cases.
A typo in a registration numberCopy and paste instead of typing, and check the names that appear before paying.
Hunting through the group chatCollect everything in one place beforehand, one entry per person.
Two people booking the same friendsAgree who covers whom, and announce bookings the moment they're confirmed.
Too many tabsOne device and one tab per registration number. The festival says using several devices or tabs will lose you your place in the queue.
A card that gets declinedCheck your limit beforehand and have a second card ready. Six deposits is a bigger payment than usual.
A phone that locks or diesCharge it, plug it in and set a longer screen lock. The tool's Keep screen on helps on your group's page.
Forgetting the balancePut the April window in your calendar straight away, and ask everyone else to as well.
Falling for a resale scamOnly buy through the official resale. Tickets carry the holder's photo and can't be transferred. See ticket scams.
