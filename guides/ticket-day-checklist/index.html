Glastonbury ticket day checklist

What to sort out two weeks before, the night before and in the minutes before the sale opens, who does what, and what to do once you're through.

By the maker of Glasto Quick Copy (about us). Facts checked 26 October 2026.

Glastonbury tickets usually sell out in under an hour. The queue is luck, but what happens after you get through isn't. This checklist covers the things you can control, in the order you'll need them.

Two weeks or more before

  • Everyone is registered with an accepted photo, and has their confirmation email.
  • You've collected every registration number and registered postcode for your group, up to six people in one booking.
  • You've agreed who books. One person, the lead booker, enters everyone's details and pays the deposits. Agree who pays back whom before the day.
  • The lead booker has a card ready with enough available for a deposit per person.
  • If your group is bigger than six, split into booking groups and agree who books for whom, so nobody is bought twice and nobody is missed.

Give everyone a job

Groups that get organised tend to split ticket morning into three simple roles. In a small group one person can do more than one.

  • Queuers. Everyone with their own registration who's free at the time. Each queues on their own device with their own registration number, and each has the group's details ready to paste.
  • The payer. Whoever gets through pays the deposits. If that might not be the same person each time, make sure every queuer has a card that can cover the group.
  • The coordinator. One person keeps the group chat tidy: they confirm when a booking is done and who it covered, so everyone else knows to stop or carry on.

A week before: a practice run

Do a short dry run. Open your group's page, tap a box, and paste it into a notes app or a message to yourself. Check it pastes all ten digits with no spaces missing. On a phone, practise switching between the ticket site and your group's page, because that's the move you'll make six times in a row on the day.

If you've never used the tool, the practice demo uses made-up people and saves nothing, so you can try it without entering anyone's details.

The night before

  • Charge your phone and laptop, and check your internet connection.
  • Make sure the lead booker knows their own registration number: recent sales have asked for it before you can even join the queue.
  • Put your group's numbers somewhere you can copy them from quickly. Scrolling a busy group chat costs time.
  • Check the official Glastonbury news page for the exact sale time and any changes to the process.
  • Check your card isn't close to its limit, and know where a second one is in case the first is declined.
  • If you want to pay with Apple Pay or Google Pay, set it up now. Keep your phone nearby for your bank's security check.
  • Set an alarm for half an hour before the sale, not five minutes.

For the exact settings on iPhone, Android and laptops, see setting up your phone or laptop for ticket day.

In the 15 minutes before

  • Be in the waiting room on the official See Tickets Glastonbury page at least 10 minutes before the sale starts. The 2027 sale FAQ said anyone arriving after the start would go to the back of the queue.
  • Use one device and one browser tab for each registration number. In recent sales extra tabs and devices on the same registration didn't give you an extra place.
  • Don't refresh once you're in the queue unless the official instructions tell you to.
  • Keep your phone screen on so it doesn't lock while you wait, and plug it in.
  • Close anything that might pop up over the page or slow your device down, like big downloads or video calls.

When you get through

The clock is running from here: in 2025 buyers had 10 minutes to complete the page with everyone's registration details, and reports said the payment page had its own, shorter timer. See paying for Glastonbury tickets for the checkout in detail.

  1. Tell the group straight away with one short message, so nobody books the same people.
  2. Enter the lead booker's details, then each person's registration number and registered postcode.
  3. Check the names that appear match your group before you continue.
  4. Pay the deposits and wait for the confirmation page.
  5. Screenshot the confirmation and the order reference, and tell the group who's booked.

Copy each number with one tap instead of typing it. Typing six ten-digit numbers under pressure is where mistakes happen.

If a name doesn't come up

If a registration number and postcode don't bring up the person you expect, don't keep retrying the same pair. Check the number with them in the group chat while you carry on with the others, and come back to that person last. It's usually a mistyped digit or an old postcode.

After you've booked

  • Put the balance payment window in your calendar, with a reminder a few days before. See deposits and balance payments.
  • Send the group what each person owes and by when.
  • If some of your group didn't get tickets, note the resale dates when they're announced. See resale and coach tickets.
