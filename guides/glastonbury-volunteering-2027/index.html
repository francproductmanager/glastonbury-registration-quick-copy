Volunteering at Glastonbury 2027: Oxfam, WaterAid and Greenpeace

How to volunteer at Glastonbury 2027 with Oxfam, WaterAid or Greenpeace: when applications open, shifts, deposits, what you get and how to get a place.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 24 November 2026.

5 min read

If you didn't get a ticket, volunteering with one of the charities that work at the festival is another way in. You work a set number of shifts and get festival entry, crew camping and meals in return. For 2027, none of the three big charity programmes is open yet: Oxfam and WaterAid open in the new year and Greenpeace in February 2027. Oxfam places go first come, first served, and Greenpeace says its places get more competitive every year.

Oxfam stewarding

Oxfam stewards check tickets and wristbands at the gates, patrol the arena and campsites, help with directions, support the accessible viewing platforms, and staff fire towers, campsite hubs and information points. Oxfam's page says 2027 applications are closed for now and will open in the new year, and sign-ups are first come, first served. If Glastonbury shows as sold out, Oxfam says places do come up again as people's plans change, so it suggests joining its mailing list.

  • Signing up: create an account on Oxfam's festivals site (you need a unique email address), choose Glastonbury, and pay the deposit. Your place is then confirmed.
  • Shifts: at weekend festivals, three shifts of eight and a quarter hours, usually a morning, an afternoon and a night shift. Oxfam warns you may get an overnight shift on the Sunday, so plan your journey home around that.
  • Deposit: one refundable deposit of £250 for weekend festivals (Boomtown is higher), which covers every festival you add at the same deposit or less. It comes back within six weeks of your last festival, as long as you attended all your shifts, were on time and returned your tabard. You can choose to donate some or all of it instead.
  • Before you go: complete every section of your profile, including a passport style photo and a referee who isn't a relative, has known you for over six months in a professional capacity, and can confirm you'll be 18 by the time you arrive. Training is needed every two years, so unless you trained with Oxfam in 2026, you train again in 2027. Not finishing your profile in time can cost you your deposit.
  • On site: you register in the Oxfam campsite with photo ID such as a driving licence or passport, collect your shift times and tabard, and attend a briefing before your first shift.
  • What you get: secure camping in the Oxfam campsite, a food voucher for each shift, free tea and coffee 24 hours a day, crew showers and toilets, and phone charging.
  • Places can't be transferred. Oxfam won't change the name on a place. If you can't go, cancel through your profile.

WaterAid

WaterAid's volunteers keep the festival's toilets and water points running. Its 2027 page says applications open early next year, and you can register interest now to hear first. Returning volunteers are reviewed first, and WaterAid says new volunteers who get a Glastonbury place are most likely to join the Loo Crew. Most volunteers complete four six-hour shifts, with a meal token for each.

Greenpeace

Greenpeace runs its own field at the festival and needs volunteers to build and run it. Its page says applications open in February 2027. You need to be over 18, on site from Tuesday 22 to Monday 28 June 2027, and able to get there under your own steam: it doesn't pay travel expenses. Aim to arrive in time for the 2pm briefing on the Tuesday.

  • Shifts: at least five shifts of six to eight hours, in most cases one a day during the festival, with meal and tea breaks. Some shifts are late at night or early in the morning.
  • Roles: all-rounders, feature volunteers, production assistants, showers crew, maintenance, the accreditation and information office, stewards and food crew. You can't pick a role: Greenpeace matches you to one on your skills and experience.
  • How places go: every application is assessed afresh and anonymously each year against a marking system, purely on what you write. Greenpeace says being part of its volunteer community already can make an application stronger.
  • What you get: secure crew camping in the middle of the festival, hot showers, three vegetarian or vegan meals a day, and a discounted crew bar.
  • Not allowed: friends, family or vehicles on the crew campsite, and there are no facilities or tickets for children.
  • Working longer: a few pre and post festival places give you an extra half shift off during the festival for every shift worked, so two shifts before the event earn a whole day off.

How to give yourself the best chance

  1. Sign up now to Oxfam's mailing list and WaterAid's interest form, and check Greenpeace's page, so you hear the moment applications open.
  2. Make your Oxfam account before places open, so you're not filling in forms while they go.
  3. Line up a referee for Oxfam now: someone who has known you for over six months through work or study, and isn't a relative.
  4. Write a strong Greenpeace application. It is marked purely on what you write, so Greenpeace says to include details and examples of why you'd be a great volunteer.
  5. Know your dates. Greenpeace needs you from Tuesday to Monday; Oxfam shifts run morning, afternoon and night.
  6. Have the deposit ready for Oxfam, as it's paid when you sign up.

Is volunteering for you?

It's real work, and Greenpeace says so plainly: hard, but very rewarding. Some shifts run through the night or start early in the morning. Oxfam has a shift swap board in its marquee, but at Glastonbury all three shifts must be swapped together and the swap made official with Oxfam, and Greenpeace volunteers can't choose their role. In return you get time to enjoy the festival when you're not on shift.

Volunteering is offered only through the charities themselves, and Oxfam doesn't let places change hands. Anyone selling a guaranteed volunteer place, or asking for money to secure one, isn't genuine. See how to spot Glastonbury ticket scams.
