Going to Glastonbury on your own: tickets, travel and help

Going to Glastonbury alone: booking one ticket, why queueing solo doesn't hurt your chances, paying your own balance, travelling alone and finding help on site.

JC

By Jonathan Connolly, who books for a group of friends every Glastonbury ticket day and built this site's tool. Facts checked 24 November 2026.

8 min read

Yes, you can go to Glastonbury on your own, and you get a ticket the same way as everyone else. You register, join the queue on sale day with your own registration, and book one ticket for yourself. You pay a £100 deposit on the day and the £308 balance in April, a general admission ticket is posted to you, and you travel by coach, train or car like anyone else. You won't be the only one: Greenpeace's tips on the festival's information page say the trains are full of festival-goers who can help you find your way once you reach the site.

This guide is about doing it alone: getting your own ticket, paying for it, getting there and finding help on site. It's built from the festival's information page, read in full on 24 November 2026, and Time Out's report on the 2027 queue. If you're going with friends, Glastonbury ticket tips covers how a group plans together.

Who can go without an adult

Anyone aged 16 or over. The festival says 16 and 17 year olds are allowed to attend unaccompanied, but strongly advises that this is decided with the consent of a parent or legal guardian. Under 16s can't enter, leave or re-enter the site without their responsible adult (13 to 15 year olds may be allowed out and back in if they wear a Parental Permission for Exit/Re-entry Wristband, which they collect together with that adult), so going alone isn't an option for them: see taking children to Glastonbury. The festival also advises anyone on an adult ticket who looks under 16 to bring ID to prove their age.

Getting a ticket on your own

Everyone aged 13 or over has to be individually registered before a sale, and that is all you need to get started. Each ticket shows the holder's photograph and is non-transferable, and security checks make sure only the person in the photo gets in. So your ticket is yours, and nobody else can use it.

  1. Register, with an up to date, passport style photo. The festival reviews photos within 48 hours in the working week, and registration is suspended before and during the main sales, so don't leave it late. See how to register for Glastonbury.
  2. Keep your registration number and registered postcode to hand. You'll be asked for them to book.
  3. Be on glastonbury.seetickets.com at least 10 minutes before the sale opens. When it starts, everyone already on the page is given a random place in the queue. Anyone who arrives later joins the back.
  4. Use one device and one tab, and don't refresh. The festival says refreshing, or using more devices or tabs, will lose you your place.
  5. When you reach the booking page, book yourself and pay. You get 5 minutes on that page, and a ticket is only allocated once payment goes through, so have your card ready, plus your phone for any security check from your bank.

Does going alone hurt your chances?

No. Time Out reported that in the 2027 coach sale everyone entered their registration number before queueing and was given a unique queue ID linked to it, so each person could join the queue only once. The festival says that in both the coach sale and the general sale, everyone already on the page when the sale begins is given a random place. Your place is random, and it's the same kind of place whether you're on your own or one of six friends.

What a group has is more people queueing: if any one of them gets through, they can book the others. That's more chances for the group, not a better place for anyone in it. You can have both: if friends are trying too, give one of them your registration number and postcode and agree that whoever gets through books you. How groups agree who books whom is in buying tickets for a group.

Paying for it yourself

How you pay depends on whether you booked yourself or a friend booked you in their group. These are the 2027 rules and prices:

You booked yourselfA friend booked you
On sale dayYou pay your own £100 depositYour friend pays your deposit with everyone else's
The balance, 1 to 7 April 2027You pay your £308 balancePaid with the rest of the order, or by you from your own card
Postage and packing£11.25 on your orderOne charge for the whole order, or £11.25 for each person who pays separately
Where your ticket goesTo your addressTo the address for the first name on the booking, unless you paid your balance separately

So on your own, a general admission ticket costs £408 (deposit and balance together) plus £11.25 postage: £419.25 in all, before any car park pass.

The festival says it is each person's responsibility to pay their own balance. If it isn't paid by the deadline, you lose the ticket and get back what you paid, less a £25 administration fee. On your own nobody else will remind you, so put the window in your calendar now. If a friend booked you, paying your balance yourself means your ticket comes straight to you, for the cost of an extra postage charge. More is in how to split Glastonbury ticket costs.

When your ticket arrives

General admission tickets are physical tickets, sent by Royal Mail in late May to June 2027, and someone has to sign for them. If yours hasn't arrived by Wednesday 16 June 2027, contact See Tickets. Moved house? Tell See Tickets, because changing your registration doesn't change the delivery address. More is in when Glastonbury tickets arrive.

Getting there alone

Most ways in are full of other festival-goers, which makes travelling alone easier than it sounds:

  • Coach. National Express runs direct coaches from over 90 places in the UK to the festival gates, and National Express says the coach will be full of your fellow festival-goers. On a ticket and coach package, travelling on the coach you booked is mandatory, and your festival ticket is handed out once the coach has left the pick-up point. Bring photo ID: you have to show it to receive your ticket. See coach tickets.
  • Train. Shuttle buses run from Castle Cary station, and Greenpeace's tips on the festival's information page say the trains are full of festival-goers who can help you find your way once you reach the site.
  • Car. You'll need a car park pass (£60, plus £3 postage where bought separately), bought with your balance in April, for general admission tickets only.

Pack only what you can carry: the festival says that however you arrive, you'll have to carry your stuff a fair distance to where you camp. And one tip for coach travellers on the festival's information page: print a spare copy of your return coach ticket and leave it in a Property Lock-up when you arrive, so you can still get home if you lose your phone or its battery dies. Routes and timings are in how to get to Glastonbury.

Camping on your own

Your ticket includes five nights' camping, and you can pitch anywhere in the camping fields. A few points from the festival's advice matter more when nobody else is minding your tent:

  • Find your campsite stewards. There's a stewards' base in every camping field, and they're the first people to go to in an emergency.
  • Make your tent easy to find, by noting the nearest numbered Fire Point or flying a flag.
  • Say hello to your neighbours. The festival suggests making friends with the people camped around you and looking out for each other's property, and Greenpeace's tips add that you can buddy up during the festival too.
  • Don't leave valuables in the tent. The free Property Lock-ups are open 24 hours.

Which fields are lively and which are quiet, and more camping tips, are in where to camp at Glastonbury.

Finding your way around

  • Information Points. There are five around the site, plus one at Worthy View for people staying there, with help on everything from music to markets. They can put you in touch with the right people in an emergency, or if you've lost something or feel unwell.
  • The free daily paper. The Glastonbury Free Press is a free paper printed on site. Greenpeace's tips on the festival's information page say it's loaded with tips on things to see and do, and that the interesting stuff is rarely on the main stages.
  • Your phone. Phone charging is free and included in your ticket, as are a programme and the festival's app.
  • Quiet time. If the crowds get too much, Greenpeace's tips suggest heading back to your tent or to a quiet space in the far reaches of the Green Fields for a while.

If you need help

Where to go for help on site. Worthy Welfare and the Samaritans are there 24 hours a day:

WhereWhat it's for
Worthy WelfarePractical problems, such as losing all your kit. Open 24 hours, and it runs lost property
Green WelfareFeeling under the weather, or wanting somewhere quiet to sit and talk to someone. At the entrance to the Green Fields
Worthy RestTime away from it all, with specialised support, advice and counselling. Tents in Silver Hayes and The Park
The SamaritansEmotional support, 24 hours a day, from their tent in the Main Markets area

In a medical emergency, go to a steward, an Information Point, a security person or a police officer, who can all summon help. If anyone makes you feel uncomfortable, the festival asks you to tell a member of staff or security as soon as possible. The police are on site 24 hours a day in the Big Ground, where you can report a crime or just ask for directions. Keeping your cash and cards safe is in money at Glastonbury.

Questions people ask

Can I buy two tickets and find someone to go with later?

No. Each ticket is for a named, registered person, and can't be passed on. See how many Glastonbury tickets you can buy.

If my friends get tickets and I don't, can I still go with them?

Only with a ticket of your own. Your next chance is the resale, which you can try on your own or with friends who also need tickets. See the 2027 resale.

What we couldn't confirm

The festival's information page has no section for people going on their own: we searched it for alone, solo, on your own and by yourself and found nothing beyond the advice above. It doesn't mention any meetups for people going alone, so we haven't suggested any.
